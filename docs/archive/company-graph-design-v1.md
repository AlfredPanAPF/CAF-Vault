# Company Intelligence Graph — System Design

**A self-growing, entity-resolved, temporal knowledge graph with agentic ingestion and discovery.**

This document consolidates the full design. Sections 2–12 describe the system as I would build it; every decision stated as a rule in those sections is one I am confident about and would defend. Section 13 separates those defensible decisions from the parameters that only real data can settle, and section 14 covers the ways the system fails if the rules are ignored.

---

## 1. What the system is

A repository of company information that grows on its own. Agents monitor sources (registries, filings, premium press, podcasts, blogs), ingest new material within seconds to minutes of publication, extract structured claims with full provenance, resolve every mention to a canonical company, and store the result as a temporal knowledge graph. A separate discovery layer generates hypotheses about non-obvious connections between entities, investigates them against the corpus, and promotes only the corroborated ones into the graph. A serving layer exposes the result through an API, an app, and alerts.

The two problems that decide whether this works are not the exciting ones:

1. **Entity resolution.** Every downstream capability depends on correctly mapping "Apple", "AAPL", "Apple Inc.", and its subsidiaries to one canonical node. Get this wrong and the graph is noise.
2. **Self-poisoning.** A system that generates its own links and then treats those links as evidence will confabulate. Within months, nothing in it can be trusted. The design prevents this mechanically, not by convention.

Everything else in this document is built around those two constraints.

---

## 2. Design principles

These are the load-bearing decisions. Each later section is an application of one or more of them.

**The claim is the atomic unit, not the fact.** Every extracted assertion is stored as a standalone record with subject, predicate, object, source, timestamps, and confidence. Edges in the graph are materialized views over claims. This gives provenance for free, lets contradictory sources coexist instead of silently overwriting each other, and makes every edge in the product explainable ("why is this link here?" has a stored answer).

**Append-only event log; the graph is rebuildable.** Raw events and claims are never mutated, only superseded. The entire graph can be re-materialized from the log. This matters because extraction models will improve: when a better extractor ships, you re-run it over history and rebuild, rather than living forever with the mistakes of version 1.

**Bitemporal from day one.** Every claim distinguishes *valid time* (when the fact became true in the world) from *observation time* (when the system learned it). A CEO change announced in March but effective in June is two different timestamps. Any system selling speed needs this distinction; retrofitting it later is painful.

**A hard firewall between asserted and inferred.** Claims extracted from sources live in the asserted layer. Links generated by discovery agents live in an inferred layer with hypothesis status. Inferred edges never serve as evidence for new inferences, and promotion into the asserted layer requires independent source-backed corroboration. This is enforced by giving investigation agents a database view that physically excludes inferred edges, not by prompt instructions.

**All entity operations are reversible.** Merging two entity records is done with `same_as` edges, never by destructive rewrite. Bad merges are guaranteed to happen; the design makes them cheap to undo.

**Budget follows the power law.** Company information flow is extremely skewed. A few hundred thousand entities generate nearly all events; hundreds of millions of registered entities generate none. The architecture makes the long tail nearly free (static registry claims, no monitoring) and concentrates compute on the head.

**Provenance is a product feature, not plumbing.** The evidence trail behind every claim and every inferred link is stored in a form the app can render. Trust is the product.

## 3. Architecture overview

The pipeline, end to end:

```
sources → monitoring agents → event bus → extraction → entity resolution
        → stores (knowledge graph | vector index | raw artifacts)
        → discovery agents → inferred layer → (corroboration) → graph
        → serving (API, app, alerts)
```

Two paths run over the same spine:

- **Fast path.** A new event is triaged by a cheap classifier within seconds. High-materiality items get lightweight extraction (headline entities, event type) and immediately trigger alerts.
- **Slow path.** The same event then goes through full extraction, resolution, claim writing, and neighborhood rescans for discovery, on the order of minutes.

The fast path is what makes the product feel instant; the slow path is what makes it correct. They never block each other.

## 4. Source and monitoring layer

**Connector classes.** Each source type gets a connector that normalizes output into a common event envelope: registries and filings (EDGAR, Companies House, and national equivalents), premium press and licensed feeds, podcasts and other audio, blogs, newsletters, and social. Podcasts go through ASR (Whisper-class) plus speaker diarization before entering the pipeline; after that step they are ordinary documents.

**Webhook-first, adaptive polling second.** Where a source offers push (webhooks, RSS with fast TTL, filing feeds), use it. Everywhere else, pollers learn each source's publishing cadence from its history: a filings feed that fires at 16:05 local time daily gets polled tightly around 16:05 and loosely otherwise; a weekly podcast gets checked around its release pattern. Polling frequency is a per-source learned schedule, not a global constant. This is the single biggest cost lever in the ingestion layer.

**Event envelope.** Everything on the bus conforms to:

```
Event {
  event_id        uuid
  source_id       uuid            // the feed/channel
  connector       string
  fetched_at      timestamp
  published_at    timestamp?      // as claimed by the source
  content_hash    sha256          // dedup across mirrors
  artifact_uri    uri             // raw bytes in the artifact store
  mime            string
  triage {
    materiality   float           // cheap-model score
    entities      [string]        // rough guesses, pre-resolution
    route         fast | slow
  }
}
```

**Deduplication happens here.** Content hashing plus near-duplicate detection (shingling or embedding similarity above a threshold) collapses syndicated copies before they cost extraction tokens. The original publication is recorded as the lineage root; mirrors attach to it. This lineage record is what the discovery verifier later uses to count independent sources.

**Raw artifact storage.** Every fetched artifact is written to object storage in per-source buckets with per-source retention configuration. Derived data (claims, embeddings) is stored separately from raw content. Whatever licensing terms end up applying to a given premium source, enforcing them becomes a bucket policy rather than a redesign.

## 5. Extraction and normalization

**Pipeline.** Parse (or transcribe) → segment → extract entity mentions → extract claims → attach provenance. Extraction is LLM-based with a tiered model strategy: a small model handles routine documents; a strong model handles dense material (filings, transcripts) and anything the small model flags as low-confidence.

**Claim schema.** The core record of the entire system:

```
Claim {
  claim_id      uuid
  subject       entity_ref            // canonical or provisional
  predicate     string                // open vocabulary at write time
  object        entity_ref | literal
  qualifiers    map                   // amount, role, jurisdiction, ...
  source_id     uuid                  // document segment of origin
  lineage_id    uuid                  // root reporting origin
  observed_at   timestamp             // when the system learned it
  valid_from    timestamp?            // when it became true, if stated
  valid_to      timestamp?
  confidence    float                 // extraction confidence
  extractor     string                // model + version
  status        asserted | superseded | retracted
}
```

Every field earns its place. `extractor` versioning enables selective re-extraction when models improve. `lineage_id` enables independent-source counting. `status` allows correction without deletion.

**Emergent schema with a gardener.** The graph runs on a minimal ontology spine of roughly 15–20 node types (Company, Person, Product, Event, Filing, Location, Security, Patent, and similar) while the predicate vocabulary stays open at write time. Extractors write whatever relationship the source expresses. A periodic gardener job then clusters predicate embeddings, canonicalizes synonyms ("acquired", "bought", "took over" map to one predicate), and promotes frequently recurring novel predicates into the spine. This is the resolution to the tension in a bottom-up design: without the gardener, an open vocabulary degrades into synonym soup and queries stop working; with a closed vocabulary, the system cannot represent relationships nobody anticipated. Emergence happens at the predicate level; queryability survives.

**Speakers are entities.** Diarized podcast speakers resolve to Person nodes. Over time each speaker accumulates a track record: how their past claims held up against later evidence. That reliability score feeds into claim confidence. The source-quality model lives inside the graph rather than beside it, and "independent analyst with a strong record" becomes a queryable property.

## 6. Entity resolution

The keystone service. It maps every mention in every document to a canonical entity, and everything downstream inherits its error rate.

**Seeding.** Canonical entities are bootstrapped from structured registries: GLEIF's LEI dataset, OpenCorporates, national company registers, ticker/exchange listings, and domain ownership. This yields a canonical spine of identifiers before a single document is processed. Long-tail entities exist as registry-seeded shells carrying only their registry claims.

**Resolution is two-stage.** First, *blocking*: cheap candidate generation using name n-grams, normalized legal-suffix stripping, domain matches, jurisdiction, and phonetic keys, narrowing millions of candidates to a handful. Second, *adjudication*: probabilistic matching on the candidate set, escalating to an LLM adjudicator only for genuinely ambiguous cases (shared names across jurisdictions, post-rebrand mentions, subsidiary vs. parent attribution). The escalation tier keeps LLM cost proportional to ambiguity, not volume.

**Reversibility rules.**

```
- Merges are expressed as same_as edges between records. No record is
  ever rewritten or deleted by a merge.
- Former names, brands, tickers, and subsidiaries are first-class alias
  nodes attached to the canonical entity, each with validity intervals.
- Every mention keeps a Mention record (surface form, document, span,
  resolved entity, resolver version, confidence) so any resolution
  decision can be audited and re-run.
```

Bad merges are the most damaging silent error in this class of system: they fuse two companies' histories and poison every query touching either. Reversible merges plus a labeled evaluation set (section 12) are the defense.

**Corporate structure.** Parent–subsidiary and brand relationships are ordinary claims with validity intervals, not baked into entity identity. Ownership changes constantly; identity should not.

## 7. Storage

Four stores, each doing one job:

**Knowledge graph.** A property graph holding entities and materialized edges. Every edge carries the claim IDs backing it, its validity interval, confidence, and an `origin` flag: `asserted` (source-backed) or `inferred` (promoted from discovery, carrying its evidence trail). The graph is a view over the claim log and can be rebuilt from it.

**Vector index.** Embeddings at two levels: document chunks (for retrieval) and entities (aggregated from the claims and text about them, for similarity search and candidate generation). Entity embeddings are recomputed incrementally as claims accumulate.

**Raw artifact store.** Object storage, per-source buckets, per-source retention, described in section 4. Nothing downstream reads raw artifacts except extraction and, on demand, the investigation agents.

**Metadata database.** Postgres for everything relational: sources, connectors, schedules, mention records, hypothesis records, budgets, audit logs.

The append-only claim log (on the bus, compacted into the metadata store) is the source of truth. Graph and vector index are derived and disposable.

## 8. Discovery layer

This is where the "creative" requirement lives, and it is designed as a funnel whose job is protecting expensive LLM reasoning from a combinatorially hopeless search space. With one million actively tracked entities there are ~10^12 candidate pairs. Nothing LLM-shaped runs against that space directly. The governing metric for the whole layer is **cost per promoted link**.

### 8.1 Candidate generation (algorithmic, continuous)

Five signal families run against the graph and vector index:

1. **Similarity gaps.** Entity pairs that are near neighbors in embedding space but distant (or disconnected) in the graph. Similarity without connection is inherently a question worth asking.
2. **Structural link prediction.** Scores from graph topology: classical measures (Adamic-Adar, neighborhood Jaccard) at minimum, a trained GNN if the payoff justifies it. These flag pairs the graph's own structure "expects" to be connected.
3. **Attribute joins that only exist post-resolution.** Shared auditors, registered agents, addresses, board members, patent citations, or the same obscure supplier named in two unrelated transcripts. These are the cheapest high-precision signals in the system and they are a direct payoff of good entity resolution.
4. **Temporal co-movement.** One entity's event stream consistently leading another's by a stable lag.
5. **Event-driven neighborhood rescans.** When a new claim lands on entity X, signals 1–4 re-run on X's local neighborhood only. This is what makes discovery continuous rather than a nightly batch: a fresh filing can produce a hypothesis within minutes of publication.

### 8.2 Triage (cheap model)

Candidates are scored: **interestingness = novelty × materiality × prior**.

Novelty is operationalized primarily as inverse co-mention frequency: if two companies constantly appear in the same documents, connecting them is not a discovery regardless of mechanism. Graph path distance is the secondary novelty term. Materiality comes from entity watch-scores and edge type (an ownership or dependency link outranks a shared-conference-panel link). Prior is the strength of the generating signal. A small model performs this pass; only the top slice of the queue proceeds to agents.

### 8.3 Hypothesis agent

Input: a serialized subgraph of *claims* (with sources, dates, confidences — not bare triples) plus explicit negative space ("no recorded relationship exists between A and B").

Output contract, strictly enforced:

```
Hypothesis {
  hypothesis_id    uuid
  type             typed relation (e.g. shared_supplier_dependency)
  subjects         [entity_ref]
  statement        structured claim template
  rationale        text
  test_plan {
    confirm        [what evidence would confirm]
    refute         [what evidence would refute]
  }
  origin           strategy_id + signal snapshot
  budget           { tokens, tool_calls }
  state            generated | triaged | investigating |
                   promoted | parked | refuted
  evidence         [claim_id]        // asserted layer only
  lineages         [lineage_id]      // independent source lines
  confidence       float
  wake_conditions  [entity + claim-type filters]
  history          audit log
}
```

A hypothesis without a test plan is discarded on the spot. The falsifiability requirement filters out most vibes-based pattern matching before any money is spent investigating it. New hypotheses are embedded and deduplicated against all prior ones, including refuted ones; negative memory is what stops the system rediscovering the same dead end weekly.

### 8.4 Investigation agent

A tool-using loop with a hard budget proportional to the triage score. Tools: graph queries, hybrid search over the corpus, targeted raw-document fetches (risk-factor sections of filings, specific transcript segments), and the ability to ask the monitoring layer to prioritize a source.

Two mechanical rules:

1. **Claim-ID grounding.** Every piece of evidence the agent returns must be a claim ID that resolves in the store. Citations that do not resolve are discarded. This is the hallucination guard.
2. **Firewall by construction.** The agent queries a view of the graph that physically contains only asserted claims. Inferred edges are invisible to it. The no-self-citation rule is enforced by the database, not the prompt.

### 8.5 Adversarial verifier

A separate prompt (ideally a separate model) whose explicit stance is to kill the hypothesis. Its most important job is **source-lineage deduplication**: two articles citing the same wire story are one source, not two. Evidence is traced through claim provenance back to publication lineage, and only independent lineages count. Promotion requires N independent lineages, with N scaled to how consequential the edge type is. The verifier's output — surviving evidence, confidence, reasoning — is stored as the edge's permanent evidence trail and is exactly what the app renders when a user asks why a link exists.

### 8.6 Lifecycle

```
generated → triaged → investigating → { promoted | parked | refuted }
```

**Promoted** edges enter the asserted graph flagged as inference-derived, with trail attached. **Refuted** hypotheses persist as negative knowledge. **Parked** is the state that makes the repository feel alive: a hypothesis with insufficient evidence registers wake conditions, effectively subscribing to its entities, and when a relevant claim arrives months later the investigation resumes with its old context intact. The system accumulates open questions, not just answers, and the ingestion stream keeps answering them.

### 8.7 Learning loop

Per candidate-generation strategy, track promoted links per dollar spent, and run a multi-armed bandit over exploration budget: strategies with better hit rates get more budget automatically. Early on, a human review queue gates promotion for high-materiality edges; those accept/reject decisions calibrate the verifier's thresholds. Once the app exists, user signals (flags, saves, evidence click-throughs) become free labels.

### 8.8 Worked example

Ingestion lands a podcast transcript in which an analyst mentions that a niche electrolyte producer supplies "most of the mid-tier battery names." Resolution maps the producer to a canonical entity. The event-driven rescan fires an attribute join: two battery companies with no graph connection now share a supplier mention. Triage scores it high (near-zero co-mention, high materiality). The hypothesis agent proposes a typed shared-dependency link with a test plan: look for the supplier in both companies' filing risk sections and any procurement claims. Investigation finds it in one filing and one additional independent transcript. The verifier counts three independent lineages and promotes at 0.72 confidence. Nobody wrote a rule about batteries or electrolytes; the link emerged bottom-up from the data.

## 9. Quality control and self-growth safety

**Contradiction detection.** Conflicting claims on the same subject–predicate (with overlapping validity intervals) are flagged into an adjudication queue rather than silently coexisting or overwriting. Resolution policies can be automatic (prefer higher-reliability lineage, prefer more recent valid_from) with escalation to human review above a materiality threshold.

**Staleness decay.** Claim confidence decays by predicate type: a headcount claim ages fast, an incorporation date does not. Decay rates are per-predicate configuration, not global.

**Source reliability inside the graph.** Publications, feeds, and individual speakers carry reliability scores computed from how their historical claims fared against later evidence. Scores feed claim confidence at extraction time and lineage weighting at verification time.

**The poisoning failure mode, restated.** If any of the following are relaxed, the system degrades: inferred edges used as evidence, promotion without independent lineages, destructive entity merges, or extraction without claim-level provenance. Every one of these produces errors that compound silently. The design treats them as invariants enforced by the data layer.

## 10. Serving layer

**API-first.** A GraphQL API over the graph (the query model maps naturally onto graph traversal), plus hybrid search combining BM25 and vector retrieval. The webapp and mobile app are thin clients over the same API.

Three UI primitives carry the product:

1. **Entity pages.** A company rendered as a timeline of claims, each expandable to its source, confidence, and lineage. Bitemporality shows up here as "as-of" views: the page can be rendered as it would have looked on any past date.
2. **Graph explorer.** Interactive neighborhood exploration with asserted and inferred edges visually distinct, and every inferred edge one tap away from its evidence trail.
3. **Alert feeds.** Subscriptions on entities, claim types, and hypothesis promotions, fed by the fast path. "Tell me the moment anything material lands on these 40 companies" is the core alerting contract.

The evidence trail is the differentiating surface. Aggregators show conclusions; this system shows its work.

## 11. Reference stack

Shapes are high-confidence; specific vendors are interchangeable within each shape.

| Layer | Reference choice | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Event bus | Kafka or Redpanda | The append-only log pattern is the commitment; either vendor serves it. |
| Raw artifacts | S3-compatible object storage | Per-source buckets and retention policies. |
| Metadata | Postgres | Mentions, hypotheses, schedules, audit. |
| Graph | Neo4j, or Postgres + Apache AGE | Start with whichever the team operates best; revisit past ~100M edges. |
| Vector index | pgvector to start, Qdrant at scale | Entity + chunk embeddings. |
| Agent orchestration | Temporal | Durable, resumable workflows fit parked hypotheses and long investigations. |
| ASR | Whisper-class + diarization (e.g. pyannote) | Podcasts become documents. |
| LLMs | Tiered: small model for triage/routine extraction, strong model for dense extraction, hypotheses, verification | The tiering pattern is the commitment; model choices will change often. |
| API | GraphQL + hybrid search (BM25 + vector) | Thin app clients. |

## 12. Phasing and metrics

**Phase 1 — the vertical slice.** 300–500 companies in a single sector, full loop end to end: monitoring, extraction, resolution, graph, discovery, a minimal entity page. Two numbers decide whether the concept works:

- **Entity resolution precision** on a labeled evaluation set of merge decisions. Starting bar: ~0.99 on merges before scaling. Precision matters more than recall here; a missed match is recoverable, a bad merge poisons queries.
- **Hypothesis precision**: the fraction of promoted links a human reviewer judges real. Starting bar: ~0.7, tightening as the verifier calibrates. Below ~0.5, the discovery layer is generating work, not value.

These bars are starting calibration points, not gospel; the point is to measure them before widening scope.

**Phase 2.** Widen sectors and source classes, turn on the strategy bandit, keep the human review queue on high-materiality promotions.

**Phase 3.** Registry-wide entity shells (the full long tail as static claims), active monitoring across the head, app launch.

**Operating metrics worth dashboarding from day one:** freshness latency per source class (fast path p50/p95; filings should alert in under a minute), extraction throughput and unit cost, ER precision/recall against the eval set, contradiction backlog size and age, hypothesis funnel conversion by stage, cost per promoted link, and parked-hypothesis wake rate (how often a wake-up leads anywhere; it measures whether the "open questions" mechanism earns its storage).

## 13. Confidence summary

**Decisions I would defend as stated:**

| Decision | Why it holds |
|---|---|
| Claims as the atomic unit, edges as views | Provenance, coexisting contradictions, explainability all fall out of it; nothing else provides them retroactively. |
| Append-only log, rebuildable graph | Extraction models will improve; re-running history is the only clean upgrade path. |
| Bitemporality from day one | Cannot be retrofitted cheaply; any speed-focused product needs it. |
| Asserted/inferred firewall, enforced in the data layer | The known failure mode of self-generating knowledge systems; prompt-level enforcement is insufficient. |
| Reversible entity merges (same_as, alias nodes, mention records) | Bad merges are certain; the only question is whether they are cheap to undo. |
| Ontology spine + open predicates + gardener | The only stable middle between rigid schemas and synonym soup. |
| Funnel economics for discovery (algorithmic → cheap model → agents) | Pairwise LLM brainstorming is combinatorially impossible; nothing about better models changes that. |
| Falsifiability contract on hypotheses | The cheapest known filter separating testable ideas from plausible mush. |
| Claim-ID grounding of all agent evidence | The only hallucination guard that verifies rather than trusts. |
| Source-lineage deduplication in verification | Syndication is pervasive; counting mirrors as sources inflates confidence system-wide. |
| Co-mention frequency as the novelty core | Directly operationalizes "links few people have made"; cheap to compute. |
| Power-law scoping (registry shells for the tail, monitoring for the head) | Matches how company information is actually distributed. |
| Fast path / slow path split | Latency and correctness have different budgets; coupling them sacrifices one. |
| Phase-1 validation on ER precision + hypothesis precision | These two numbers gate everything downstream; measuring anything else first is procrastination. |

**Parameters that only real data can settle:**

The gardener's clustering thresholds and cadence. The triage weighting between novelty, materiality, and prior. Verifier lineage thresholds (N) per edge type. Whether a GNN beats classical link prediction enough to justify its pipeline. The graph store choice at target scale. Actual hypothesis hit rates and cost per promoted link (the business case rests on these and cannot be estimated honestly in advance). ASR and diarization quality on low-production niche podcasts. Alias coverage in jurisdictions with weak registries. How often parked hypotheses wake usefully rather than accumulating as debt. Model selection at every tier, which will change monthly regardless of what is written here.

## 14. Risks and failure modes

**Graph poisoning.** Covered throughout; the firewall, lineage counting, and claim grounding exist for this. The residual risk is social: under delivery pressure, someone relaxes an invariant "temporarily." Treat the invariants as schema, not policy.

**Silent merge corruption.** A wrong same_as edge fuses two companies' histories. Mitigation: reversibility, the labeled eval set, and alerting on post-merge contradiction spikes (a merged entity suddenly contradicting itself is the symptom).

**Cost blowout.** Agent layers fail economically before they fail technically. Mitigations: hard budgets per hypothesis, the strategy bandit, tiered models, adaptive polling, and cost-per-promoted-link on the main dashboard rather than buried in billing.

**Circular sourcing.** Wire-service syndication makes one report look like ten. Lineage tracking at ingestion is the fix, and it must happen at ingestion; lineage is nearly impossible to reconstruct later.

**Stale confidence.** Old claims presented as current. Per-predicate decay plus as-of rendering in the UI keep the product honest about what it knew and when.

**Source access fragility.** Premium sources change terms, formats, and availability. The per-source bucket and retention design localizes the blast radius of any single source's constraints to configuration; connector isolation localizes format breakage. (Licensing strategy itself is out of scope here, per the project owner.)

---

*End of design. The two prompts that will absorb the most iteration are the hypothesis agent's (typed, falsifiable output rather than plausible mush) and the verifier's (skepticism calibrated so it neither rubber-stamps nor kills everything). A small labeled eval set for each, built during phase 1, is the highest-leverage early investment.*
